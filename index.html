What this shows

The human ribosome with the SUMO2/3 acceptor lysines of its proteins, on a choice of structures: the mature 80S, translating and idle ribosomes, two collided ribosomes, and assembly intermediates of both subunits. On the structure shown now (, PDB ) sites on ribosomal proteins are listed; of them have coordinates and are drawn as enlarged, coloured residues. Each site carries its response to proteasome inhibition (MG132) and to heat shock, and a description of where the lysine sits in the particle.

Eight steps

  1. Choose a structure under Structure; the page opens on the mature 80S. Structures.
  2. Look around. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. All controls.
  3. Choose what the colours mean under Colour the sites by. The default is the MG132 fold change. Colourings.
  4. Jump to a standard view — the 40S or 60S solvent side, the two faces between the subunits, top, bottom. Views.
  5. Find a site. Type a protein name above the table, sort by a column, click a row: the structure centres on that lysine and its details appear below the table. Table.
  6. Ask whether a SUMO fits. The colouring Room for SUMO shows at which sites a SUMO has space; the details of a site can draw one there. Room for SUMO.
  7. Share what you see. Copy link gives an address that opens the same structure, colouring, filters, site and view. Sharing a view.
  8. Add your own numbers. Load a table… colours proteins or sites with values from a file on your computer; the file stays on your computer. Your data.

Reading the picture

Every atom is drawn as a sphere. rRNA is light grey, the proteins of the small (40S) subunit sand, those of the large (60S) subunit blue-grey. Factors bound to the ribosome are olive, tRNA teal, mRNA black. SUMO sites are drawn larger and in colour, so that a single lysine remains visible when the whole ribosome is in view.

The enlargement is a marker, not the size of the residue. A site that is buried inside the particle shows only where its marker reaches the surface, or not at all; the table lists it all the same, and selecting it moves the view to its position.

Choosing a structure

A structure is one state of the ribosome, and a lysine that is enclosed in one state can be free in another. The Structure menu therefore offers several, all of the human ribosome and all from the PDB:

Choosing a structure replaces the picture, the site table and the Statistics window. The colouring, the selected site and a table of your own carry over. The address then holds s= and the PDB code (for example ?s=8G60), beside everything else that is chosen, and can be shared (Sharing a view); without it the page opens on the mature 80S.

The mature 80S is the reference: it has the highest resolution, and the statistics on regions, clustering and hotspots are computed on it. The decoding 80S adds mRNA, two tRNAs and eEF1A, and resolves the L1 stalk (RPL10A), RPL10 and part of the P stalk base (RPL12), which the mature structure does not. The idle 80S carries eEF2 and SERBP1, the state of ribosomes that are not translating. The collided disome is a stalled ribosome with the next ribosome run into it, bound by the kinase ZAK and by EDF1. The pre-60S and pre-40S particles are subunits in the making, with assembly factors in place of what is still to come.

One site in several structures

Each structure lists the sites of the ribosomal proteins it contains. The numbers differ because the structures differ: a precursor of the large subunit has no 40S proteins, the decoding ribosome has stalk proteins that the mature structure lacks, and the disome holds every protein twice, so each of its sites appears once for the stalled and once for the collided ribosome.

The detail card of a selected site has a line Elsewhere: the environment of the same lysine in each of the other structures, or not modelled where the residue has no coordinates there, or protein absent. A name in that line opens that structure with the same site selected and in view. For the disome the line gives the better-resolved of the two copies.

The filter resolved here, not in the mature 80S lists what a structure adds to the reference.

Factors, tRNA and mRNA

Everything in a structure that is not ribosomal protein or rRNA is a partner: translation and assembly factors (olive), tRNA (teal), mRNA (black). The checkbox under the Structure menu takes them out of the picture, which uncovers the sites beneath them; in the nucleolar pre-60S, factors cover much of the surface.

In the disome the collided ribosome is drawn in paler tones than the stalled one, so that the two can be told apart.

A site counts as near a partner when its side-chain nitrogen (NZ) is within Å of an atom of a factor, a tRNA, the mRNA or, in the disome, of a protein or rRNA of the other ribosome. The nearest partner and the distance are in the detail card (Partner), in the tooltip, and can be coloured and filtered. A site is covered by a partner when its NZ would be exposed on the ribosome alone and is enclosed once the partners are in place.

Orientation

Every single-particle structure is superposed on the mature 80S through the phosphorus atoms of the rRNA they share, and its view buttons use the directions of the mature 80S. The same button therefore shows the same face of the ribosome in every structure. In a precursor of one subunit, a view named after the other subunit looks at the face where that subunit will bind.

The disome has views of its own; see the standard views.

Comparing with the mature 80S

Two tools set a structure against the reference. The colouring Environment in the mature 80S paints each site with the environment the same lysine has in the mature ribosome; switching between it and Lysine environment shows what changes. The filter exposed here, enclosed in the mature 80S lists the sites that are exposed in the structure shown and buried or in contact with rRNA in the mature one.

For the assembly intermediates the Statistics window counts these cases and compares them with the other lysines: see Against the mature 80S.

The selected site

Selecting a row in the table centres the view on that lysine and outlines it in green. The outline stays until another site is selected.

MG132 fold change

The change of SUMO2/3 modification of the site after proteasome inhibition with MG132, as log2 fold change against control (HEK293 cells; Hendriks et al. 2018). A value of 3 is an 8-fold increase.

Only sites with q < 0.05 are coloured, from pale sand (small change) through rose to dark indigo (large change). The scale runs from 0 to 8; larger values are shown in the colour for 8. Sites that do not change significantly are dark grey. of the sites change significantly, and every one of them increases.

Z-score in control, MG132 or heat shock

The Z-score places a site's SUMOylation in one condition relative to the same site in the other conditions: 0 is the site's mean across control, MG132 and heat shock, positive values (rose) are above it, negative values (indigo) below, in units of the site's standard deviation. Values are those of the published data set; the scale runs from −1.5 to 1.5.

Switching between the three Z-score colourings shows the same sites under each condition: nearly all are low in control and rise with MG132; a subset rises with heat shock. A Z-score compares conditions within one site. It does not compare one site with another; the fold change does.

Sites without quantification are dark grey.

Room for SUMO

Near a partner

Offered when the structure holds partners. Sites within Å of a factor are wine red, of a tRNA green, of the mRNA indigo, and of the other ribosome of a disome rose; all other sites are dark grey. Region

Colours each site by the part of the ribosome it lies in: 40S head, 40S body, the central protuberance of the 60S, or the 60S body. How the regions are drawn is described under Regions.

Hotspots

Shows the groups of MG132-responsive sites that lie close together, each group in its own colour; all other sites are dark grey. A hotspot is a description of where the modification is dense, not a tested unit: see Hotspots. The hotspots are defined on the mature 80S and keep their number and colour in every structure.

Other modifications

Marks the sites whose lysine UniProt also records as carrying another modification: ubiquitin, an acetyl or a succinyl group. A site with several is shown in the colour of the first of these that applies; the detail card lists all of them. Sites without such a record are dark grey. See Other modifications.

q-values and significance

q is the false-discovery-rate estimate published with the data (three replicates per condition). A site is called significant in a condition when q < 0.05. A q of 0 is printed as 0: it is the value in the source, meaning smaller than the resolution of the estimate.

Colouring the proteins

By default the proteins are tinted by subunit. After a table with per-protein values has been loaded, Colour the proteins by appears and each protein can be coloured by one of its columns instead; the site colouring stays independent, so a protein-level measurement and the site-level response can be read together. Protein values use green scales (purple to green when they span zero) to keep them apart from the site colours. See colour scales for your data.

The four environments

EnvironmentDefinitionSites
exposedthe side-chain nitrogen (NZ) has at least 10 Å² of solvent-accessible surface in the structure shown and is not within 4 Å of rRNA
rRNA contactNZ lies within 4 Å of an rRNA atom, typically a phosphate
buriedneither: NZ has less than 10 Å² of accessible surface and no rRNA contact; it is covered by protein, or by a partner bound to the ribosome
not modelledthe residue is part of the protein in the structure but has no coordinates: it lies in a tail or loop too mobile to be resolved

What the environment tells you

An exposed lysine, and one in an unresolved mobile segment, can be reached on the particle. A lysine whose nitrogen is enclosed by rRNA or by neighbouring proteins cannot carry SUMO in the particle as it is modelled here; a modification found on it points to the protein outside this particle — free, during assembly or disassembly — or to another state of the ribosome. The other structures show some of these states: the line Elsewhere of the detail card gives the environment of the same lysine in each of them.

The detail card gives the accessible surface of NZ twice: in the structure shown and in the protein taken on its own. A lysine that is exposed in the free protein and enclosed in the ribosome is covered by the assembly.

How it is computed

From the atomic model, without water, ions and ligands. Accessible surface: a probe of 1.4 Å radius rolled over all protein and RNA atoms of the structure, partners included (FreeSASA). rRNA contact: the smallest distance from NZ to any rRNA atom. "In the free protein" uses the atoms of that one protein chain only, in the conformation it has in the ribosome.

Limits

Each structure is one state of the ribosome, prepared and selected for a particular question; surfaces that are free in it can be occupied in other states, and flexible regions move. The mature 80S is vacant: no mRNA, tRNA or factors. The thresholds (4 Å, 10 Å²) are conventions; the detail card shows the underlying numbers. SUMO is a protein of about 11 kDa, far larger than the probe: "exposed" means reachable by solvent, which is necessary for modification but does not prove that the conjugation machinery fits.

What it measures

An exposed lysine can be reached by water. SUMO is a protein of 93 residues, about 11 kDa, and the lysine environment does not say whether it fits. Room for SUMO asks exactly that. Picture a SUMO held by its tail at the lysine and turned in every direction, closer and farther: in how many of these positions does it bump into nothing? The folded core of SUMO2 is placed at the lysine in such ways, and the share of placements that overlap nothing is the measure.

ClassShare of placements that fitReading
roomat least a SUMO can sit at this lysine in the structure as it is
tightbetween the twoa few placements fit; a small movement decides
nonefewer than this lysine cannot carry SUMO in this state unless the structure gives way

For a sense of scale: a lysine on the open surface of a protein reaches about 30 to 40 % (RanGAP1's SUMO site alone reaches ); half of all placements would need the lysine on a stalk. On a ribosome, with neighbours on most sides, a few per cent is already a comfortable fit. An exposed lysine can have no room when its nitrogen is open to water at the bottom of a cleft, and a lysine classed buried can have room when its nitrogen sits at the surface next to what it touches.

The share is computed for every structure of the menu, so one site can have room in an assembly intermediate and none in the mature ribosome. The detail card of a site gives the share, and the colouring Room for SUMO paints the classes.

How it is computed

The core of SUMO2 (residues 17–88, PDB ) is rigid. The five residues after it and the bond to the lysine form a flexible tether. A placement fits when three things hold: the last residue of the core lies to Å from the lysine's nitrogen, the span of the five tail residues from compact to stretched; the tether has a way out, that is, there is room for the glycine bonded to the nitrogen and the straight line from there to the core is free; and no backbone or Cβ atom of the core comes within Å of an atom of the structure. The placements are tether directions × tether lengths × orientations of the core, the same set at every lysine, so that the shares compare between lysines and between structures.

The test is lenient: side chains of SUMO are not tested, because they can rearrange. A class none is therefore the firm statement; a class room says that the backbone of SUMO has space, which is necessary, not sufficient.

The set of placements is finite. The class boundaries are chosen so that this hardly matters: with four times as many placements the class room stays the same for all but a handful of lysines, and a few per cent of the lysines classed none move to tight. A single lysine near a boundary can be on either side; the shares themselves are the better guide there.

The model was tested on a crystal structure of SUMO2 conjugated to RanGAP1 (PDB 3UIO): the SUMO seen there passes the test, its tether measures Å, and of the placements fit with the enzymes of that structure bound, on RanGAP1 alone.

What takes the room away

The same placements are tested four times, each time with less of the structure: as it is; without the partners (factors, tRNA, mRNA); without the other ribosome of a disome; and on the lysine's own subunit alone. Each step can only add room, so the four shares in the detail card never decrease from one to the next; where a share does not change, the thing removed was not in the way, and the card leaves it out. When a site has no room in the structure as it is and gains room once something is taken away, that something is named: room taken by its partners, by the other ribosome, by the other subunit. If more than one is in the way, the first of these in that order whose removal gives room is named. A site that has no room even on its own subunit is enclosed by its own subunit, and nothing is named. The third menu above the table filters by it.

A site whose room the other subunit takes can carry SUMO on a free subunit but not in the assembled ribosome: a SUMO there stands in the way of the subunits joining, or has to be removed first. The same holds for the factor or the neighbouring ribosome.

Showing a SUMO

In the detail card of a site with a possible placement, Show a SUMO here draws the core of SUMO2 in green at one of the placements that fit, one from the middle of the possible directions. It is an example, not a prediction: with sites and thousands of possible placements each, the picture shows where a SUMO can be, not where it is. The tether is not drawn, so the SUMO sits up to Å from the lysine.

For a site whose room is taken, the button draws the SUMO where it would sit without what is in its way; it then overlaps that partner, ribosome or subunit in the picture. One SUMO is drawn at a time; choosing another structure removes it.

Limits

The structures are rigid here; parts that move in the cell can make room or take it. Segments without coordinates are not there to collide with, so room near a mobile tail is overestimated. One SUMO is placed; a chain of several needs more space, so none for one SUMO holds for a chain as well. Room for SUMO does not mean room for the enzymes that attach it.

What is compared, and where the numbers come from

A SUMO site is a lysine. Every question in the Statistics window is therefore asked against the other lysines of the same proteins: sites on proteins against lysines of those proteins that are not sites.

The numbers are computed once, by the analysis that produces this viewer's data, from the published site catalogue, the structures and UniProt. The window shows these stored results; it does not recompute them when the table is filtered or a table of your own is loaded.

The window follows the structure shown. For the mature 80S it holds the five analyses described in the entries below; for the other structures it holds what that state adds: see the other structures.

Statistics of the other structures

This structure counts, per ribosome, the ribosomal proteins present, the sites listed and those with coordinates, their environments, and the sites covered by a partner; it names the sites that have coordinates here and not in the mature 80S. For single particles it gives the fit of the superposition on the mature 80S (root-mean-square distance of the rRNA phosphorus atoms used).

A click on a site in any table of the window closes it and selects that site.

Near the partners

For each kind of partner in the structure, the table counts the SUMO sites and the other lysines of the same proteins whose NZ lies within Å of it, among those with coordinates, and gives the odds ratio with its 95 % interval and p-value from Fisher's exact test. q adjusts the p-values of all such tests across all structures (Benjamini–Hochberg). In the disome each ribosome is tested separately. The second table lists the sites concerned.

A lysine next to a factor in a structure can be covered by that factor, or can be where the factor is modified from; the distance alone does not tell which. The counts are small, and the sites of one protein (several of them on RACK1 in the disome) are not independent.

Against the mature 80S

For the assembly intermediates. The lysines whose NZ is enclosed in the mature ribosome are followed into the precursor: exposed there, still enclosed, not modelled, or on a protein that has not yet joined the particle. The first table gives these counts for the SUMO sites and for the other lysines of the proteins of that subunit; the line below it tests, among the lysines resolved in both, whether the SUMO sites are exposed more often than the others (Fisher's exact test). The last table lists the SUMO sites that the precursor exposes.

An enclosed site that is exposed in a precursor could be modified before the ribosome is complete. A precursor structure is one snapshot of a pathway with many intermediates, and the earliest ones are not covered.

Modified lysines against the other lysines

For a yes/no property (modelled, in contact with rRNA, NZ enclosed, at the subunit interface) the table gives the share of SUMO sites and of other lysines that have it, and the odds ratio with its 95 % interval from Fisher's exact test. For a measured quantity (accessible surface of NZ) it gives the two medians and the shift between the groups (Hodges–Lehmann estimate, Wilcoxon rank-sum test).

q is the p-value adjusted for the six questions of a comparison (Benjamini–Hochberg). Rows with q < 0.05 are tinted. The second table asks the same questions of the MG132-responsive sites against the sites that do not respond.

Subunits and regions

The regions are derived from the structure. The 40S head is the stretch of 18S rRNA that has the fewest contacts with the rest of the 18S — nucleotides –, the 3′ major domain — and a 40S lysine belongs to the head when the nearest 18S nucleotide lies in that stretch; the other 40S lysines form the 40S body. A 60S lysine within Å of the 5S rRNA belongs to the central protuberance, the others to the 60S body. A lysine is at the subunit interface when its NZ is within Å of the other subunit.

The table gives, per region, the share of its lysines that are SUMO sites (with a 95 % Wilson interval), the odds ratio against all other lysines, the number of responsive sites and their median fold change. The subunit table compares 40S and 60S directly. Because the sites of one protein are not independent, the fold change is also tested with one value per protein (its median).

Spatial clustering

The test counts the pairs of sites whose NZ atoms are within Å of each other and compares the count with random draws of as many lysines. Three ways of drawing answer three questions:

Null modelDrawn fromQuestion
any lysineall modelled lysines of the ribosomeAre the sites closer together than lysines in general?
same proteinsthe lysines of each protein, as many as that protein has sitesGiven which proteins are modified, are the sites closer together than the lysines of those proteins are anyway?
among the sitesthe SUMO sites, as many as respondAre the responsive sites closer together than SUMO sites in general?

"Expected" is the mean of the draws, with the range that holds 95 % of them. p is the share of draws that reach the observed value. The mean distance to the nearest other site and the number of sites in hotspots are tested the same way.

Hotspots

A hotspot is a group of at least MG132-responsive sites in which every site has another within Å (NZ to NZ). Hotspots are numbered by size. Show colours the sites by hotspot, lists the sites of that hotspot in the table and turns the structure to it.

Hotspots describe where the modification is densest. Whether there are more sites in hotspots than chance places there is the last line of the clustering table.

Other modifications

From the cross-link and modified-residue records of UniProt for the proteins of the structure. The table gives, per modification, the number of lysines that carry it, how many of them are SUMO sites, and the odds ratio against the lysines without it. UniProt's own SUMO records are not used here: they largely come from the same kind of experiment as the site catalogue.

A lysine found modified in one proteomics experiment is more likely to be found in another, whatever the modifier. An overlap therefore shows that the same lysines are used; it does not show that one modification depends on the other.

Room for SUMO

The first table counts the classes room, tight and none among the MG132-responsive sites, the other sites and the other lysines of the same proteins, for lysines with a resolved side chain in the structure shown. The second gives the classes of the sites by the environment of their lysine: an exposed lysine need not have room for a SUMO. For the mature 80S a third table tests the differences (Fisher's exact test). The last table lists the sites that have room only when the partners, the other ribosome or the other subunit are taken away, with the share of placements that fit with and without it; a click on a row selects the site.

Definitions and the test of the model: how it is computed.

Limits

Sites on one protein are not independent observations, so a single heavily modified protein can carry a result; the tests "with proteins as units" guard against this where it matters. Lysines that mass spectrometry cannot detect are counted as unmodified. Each structure is one state of the ribosome. A p-value near 0.05 in a table of many tests is weak evidence.

How rows are matched

A row without a position is a protein value and colours the whole protein. A row with a position is a site value and colours that lysine if it is one of the listed SUMO sites. After loading, the box reports how many proteins and sites were matched, how many rows name a protein that is not in this structure, and how many positions are not among the listed sites. When another structure is chosen the table is matched again; factors of the structure can be coloured as well, by gene symbol or accession. In the disome a value colours both copies.

One table may contain both kinds of rows. If a protein or site occurs twice, the last row counts.

A column counts as numeric when at least 80 % of its filled cells are numbers. Proteins use green scales, sites the sand-to-indigo and indigo-to-rose scales of the built-in colourings. Proteins without a value keep their subunit tint; sites without a value are dark grey. The legend under each control shows the scale in use.

Where your values appear

In the structure and its legend; as an extra, sortable column of the site table (the site's own value, or its protein's); and in the detail card of a selected site, which lists all columns. Remove takes the table out again.

Limits

One table at a time. Site values are shown for the listed SUMO sites only, not for other lysines. Proteins that are absent from the structure shown cannot be coloured; which proteins a structure lacks is under what is not shown.

Structures

All structures are cryo-EM models of the human ribosome from the PDB; the table under Choosing a structure lists them with their PDB codes, which link to the entries and their publications. The reference is PDB 8QOI: human 80S ribosome at 1.9 Å (Holvec S et al., Nat Struct Mol Biol 31:1251, 2024); 75 proteins and four rRNAs. Your browser fetches the coordinates from the PDB each time; they are not stored with this page.

Which chains are ribosomal proteins, which are factors, and which RNA is rRNA, tRNA or mRNA is read from the PDB entry. Chains for which an entry records no UniProt accession are identified by their sequence against the proteins of the reference.

Placing sites on the structure

Positions are UniProt sequence positions. They are converted to residues of a structure through the alignment that the PDB entry records for each chain, and every placed site was checked against the published sequence window (the seven residues on either side of the lysine agree with the structure).

What is not shown

A structure lists the sites of the proteins it contains. The mature 80S lacks the mobile stalks (uL1/RPL10A, uL10/RPLP0, P1, uL11/RPL12, uL16/RPL10) and the paralogues RPL7L1 and RPL26L1; the decoding 80S resolves RPL10A, RPL12 and RPL10, and the pre-60S particles hold RPL7L1. The P-stalk proteins RPLP0 and RPLP1 have no side-chain coordinates in any of the structures, and RPL26L1 is in none. Not listed anywhere: lysines in the ubiquitin moieties of RPS27A and UBA52, which are cleaved off before these proteins enter the ribosome, and sites of non-ribosomal proteins, the factors included.
