What this shows

The human 80S ribosome with the SUMO2/3 acceptor lysines of its proteins. sites on ribosomal proteins are listed; of them have coordinates in the structure and are drawn as enlarged, coloured residues. Each site carries its response to proteasome inhibition (MG132) and to heat shock, and a description of where the lysine sits in the assembled ribosome.

Five steps

  1. Look around. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. All controls.
  2. Choose what the colours mean under Colour the sites by. The default is the MG132 fold change. Colourings.
  3. Jump to a standard view — the 40S or 60S solvent side, the two faces between the subunits, top, bottom. Views.
  4. Find a site. Type a protein name above the table, sort by a column, click a row: the structure centres on that lysine and its details appear below the table. Table.
  5. Add your own numbers. Load a table… colours proteins or sites with values from a file on your computer; the file stays on your computer. Your data.

Reading the picture

Every atom is drawn as a sphere. rRNA is light grey, the proteins of the small (40S) subunit sand, those of the large (60S) subunit blue-grey. SUMO sites are drawn larger and in colour, so that a single lysine remains visible when the whole ribosome is in view.

The enlargement is a marker, not the size of the residue. A site that is buried inside the particle shows only where its marker reaches the surface, or not at all; the table lists it all the same, and selecting it moves the view to its position.

The selected site

Selecting a row in the table centres the view on that lysine and outlines it in green. The outline stays until another site is selected.

MG132 fold change

The change of SUMO2/3 modification of the site after proteasome inhibition with MG132, as log2 fold change against control (HEK293 cells; Hendriks et al. 2018). A value of 3 is an 8-fold increase.

Only sites with q < 0.05 are coloured, from pale sand (small change) through rose to dark indigo (large change). The scale runs from 0 to 8; larger values are shown in the colour for 8. Sites that do not change significantly are dark grey. of the sites change significantly, and every one of them increases.

Z-score in control, MG132 or heat shock

The Z-score places a site's SUMOylation in one condition relative to the same site in the other conditions: 0 is the site's mean across control, MG132 and heat shock, positive values (rose) are above it, negative values (indigo) below, in units of the site's standard deviation. Values are those of the published data set; the scale runs from −1.5 to 1.5.

Switching between the three Z-score colourings shows the same sites under each condition: nearly all are low in control and rise with MG132; a subset rises with heat shock. A Z-score compares conditions within one site. It does not compare one site with another; the fold change does.

Sites without quantification are dark grey.

Lysine environment

Colours each site by where its lysine sits in the assembled ribosome: exposed, in contact with rRNA, or buried. The definitions are under Lysine environment.

q-values and significance

q is the false-discovery-rate estimate published with the data (three replicates per condition). A site is called significant in a condition when q < 0.05. A q of 0 is printed as 0: it is the value in the source, meaning smaller than the resolution of the estimate.

Colouring the proteins

By default the proteins are tinted by subunit. After a table with per-protein values has been loaded, Colour the proteins by appears and each protein can be coloured by one of its columns instead; the site colouring stays independent, so a protein-level measurement and the site-level response can be read together. Protein values use green scales (purple to green when they span zero) to keep them apart from the site colours. See colour scales for your data.

Your data on the sites

When a loaded table has rows with a position, its columns appear as additional site colourings named Your data: column. Sites without a value in that column are dark grey. See Your data.

The four environments

EnvironmentDefinitionSites
exposedthe side-chain nitrogen (NZ) has at least 10 Å² of solvent-accessible surface in the assembled ribosome and is not within 4 Å of rRNA
rRNA contactNZ lies within 4 Å of an rRNA atom, typically a phosphate
buriedneither: NZ has less than 10 Å² of accessible surface and no rRNA contact; it is covered by protein
not modelledthe residue is part of the protein in the structure but has no coordinates: it lies in a tail or loop too mobile to be resolved

What the environment tells you

An exposed lysine, and one in an unresolved mobile segment, can be reached on an assembled ribosome. A lysine whose nitrogen is enclosed by rRNA or by neighbouring proteins cannot carry SUMO in the particle as it is modelled here; a modification found on it points to the protein outside a mature ribosome — free, during assembly or disassembly — or to a state of the ribosome that differs locally from this structure.

The detail card gives the accessible surface of NZ twice: in the assembled ribosome and in the protein taken on its own. A lysine that is exposed in the free protein and enclosed in the ribosome is covered by the assembly.

How it is computed

From the atomic model, without water, ions and ligands. Accessible surface: a probe of 1.4 Å radius rolled over all protein and rRNA atoms (FreeSASA). rRNA contact: the smallest distance from NZ to any rRNA atom. "In the free protein" uses the atoms of that one protein chain only, in the conformation it has in the ribosome.

Limits

The structure is one state of the ribosome: a vacant 80S without mRNA, tRNA or factors. Surfaces that are free here can be occupied in other states, and flexible regions move. The thresholds (4 Å, 10 Å²) are conventions; the detail card shows the underlying numbers. SUMO is a protein of about 11 kDa, far larger than the probe: "exposed" means reachable by solvent, which is necessary for modification but does not prove that the conjugation machinery fits.

Where your file goes

Nowhere. Load a table… hands the file to this page, which reads it inside your browser. It is not sent to a server, not written to the browser's storage, and it is gone when the page is reloaded or closed. The page itself only downloads its own files and the structure from the PDB.

File format

A text table with one header row: CSV, TSV or semicolon-separated; the separator is detected. Decimal points or decimal commas. Drag the file onto the Your data box or use the button. Download an example.

How rows are matched

A row without a position is a protein value and colours the whole protein. A row with a position is a site value and colours that lysine if it is one of the listed SUMO sites. After loading, the box reports how many proteins and sites were matched, how many rows name a protein that is not in this structure, and how many positions are not among the listed sites.

One table may contain both kinds of rows. If a protein or site occurs twice, the last row counts.

A column counts as numeric when at least 80 % of its filled cells are numbers. Proteins use green scales, sites the sand-to-indigo and indigo-to-rose scales of the built-in colourings. Proteins without a value keep their subunit tint; sites without a value are dark grey. The legend under each control shows the scale in use.

Where your values appear

In the structure and its legend; as an extra, sortable column of the site table (the site's own value, or its protein's); and in the detail card of a selected site, which lists all columns. Remove takes the table out again.

Limits

One table at a time. Site values are shown for the listed SUMO sites only, not for other lysines. Proteins that are absent from the structure — the mobile stalks, see what is not shown — cannot be coloured.

Sites and quantification

Hendriks IA, Lyon D, Su D, Skotte NH, Daniel JA, Jensen LJ, Nielsen ML. Site-specific characterization of endogenous SUMOylation across species and organs. Nat Commun 9:2456 (2018), Supplementary Data 1, CC BY 4.0. Endogenous SUMO2/3 sites of HEK293 cells in control, after MG132 and after heat shock, in triplicate; Z-scores, log2 fold changes and q-values are used as published. The viewer shows the sites of the cytosolic ribosomal proteins that are part of the structure.

Structure

PDB 8QOI: human 80S ribosome by cryo-EM at 1.9 Å (Holvec S et al., Nat Struct Mol Biol 31:1251, 2024); 75 proteins and four rRNAs. Your browser fetches the coordinates from the PDB each time; they are not stored with this page.

Placing sites on the structure

Positions are UniProt sequence positions. They are converted to residues of the structure through the alignment that the PDB entry records for each chain, and every placed site was checked against the published sequence window (the seven residues on either side of the lysine agree with the structure).

What is not shown

Proteins that are not part of 8QOI: the mobile stalks (uL1/RPL10A, uL10/RPLP0, P1, uL11/RPL12, uL16/RPL10) and the paralogues RPL7L1 and RPL26L1; their SUMO sites are not listed. Lysines in the ubiquitin moieties of RPS27A and UBA52, which are cleaved off before these proteins enter the ribosome. Sites of non-ribosomal proteins.
